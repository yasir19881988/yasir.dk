From Founder to Job Seeker: The Reality Check After 16 Years

Moving from self-employment to the job market is a far bigger shift than I expected.

For 16 years, I ran my own business. I was used to making decisions, solving problems in the moment, and seeing a direct connection between effort and outcome. Becoming a job seeker has placed me in a completely different world, and it has been a humbling experience.

The Endless Cycle of Standard Rejections

What surprised me most was the amount of time and energy a serious job search requires. Finding roles that genuinely interest you, tailoring each application, and putting a piece of yourself into every attempt can quickly become a full-time job in its own right.

The hardest part is investing hours in an application for a role where you know you could make a real difference, only to receive the same automated rejection email a few weeks later.

I have asked myself why more times than I can count. I know I can do the work. I bring 16 years of hands-on business experience, resilience, and a strong drive to succeed. Yet communicating that clearly in a two-page CV is far more difficult than I imagined.

Standing Out Among 300 Applicants

The reality of the job market is demanding. When hundreds of people apply for the same role, it is difficult to become the person who stands out.

Perhaps enthusiasm and the willingness to give everything to a new company do not translate well on paper. Or perhaps automated HR systems prioritise exact job titles and formal qualifications, missing the wider value a former business owner can bring. Whatever the reason, the distance between knowing you can do the job and being given the chance to prove it in an interview can feel overwhelming.

The Breakthrough: Starting as a Temp

After meeting that wall, I decided that waiting at home was not an option. I needed to get back out there, roll up my sleeves, and put my skills to work.

Fortunately, I secured a temporary assignment. It gave me a way to get my foot in the door and return to practical work. On my first day, I did not know what it would feel like to be one person among 260 employees. I quickly learned the rhythms of a larger workplace: colleagues you connect with immediately, others you greet warmly and work with professionally.

The environment proved to be remarkably professional, with welcoming colleagues and supportive management. From day one, I focused on supporting the team and doing the work properly. Before long, I could see that I was maintaining a solid pace. Some experienced colleagues were faster, but my overall workflow was strong and my error rate consistently low. My manager noticed.

At my own request, I was given more complex and engaging tasks. That mattered to me. It made the work feel challenging, useful, and meaningful.

Rethinking the Hiring Process

I am not working full-time, but I am genuinely grateful for the opportunity. It allowed me to prove, if not to anyone else, then at least to myself, that I can thrive as an employee, complete work efficiently, and continue growing within a structured company.

At the same time, the experience has made me reflect on the traditional job hunt. Spending countless hours crafting applications increasingly feels outdated. Why not turn the process around? Candidates could maintain clear profiles that show their real-world experience, enabling employers to approach people based on demonstrated capabilities.

Does a polished application truly demonstrate a candidate's value? We are already in an era where AI produces a large share of CVs and cover letters. Employers are rapidly adopting automation and AI themselves, yet job seekers are still judged heavily on their ability to write an idealised document. How do you demonstrate work ethic, adaptability, and genuine drive on a single A4 page? The traditional recruitment process still struggles to answer that question.

Experience, work ethic, and adaptability are difficult to quantify in a hiring algorithm. Yet they are often the qualities that make someone exceptional, whether on the shop floor or in the office. My journey from running a business to taking a temporary role has confirmed one thing: real capability shows up in action, not on paper.

In a hiring world increasingly shaped by AI filters and polished cover letters, how can companies avoid overlooking the genuine drive and real-world experience that an A4 sheet will never reveal?
